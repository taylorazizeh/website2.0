Kelp gulls *Larus dominicanus* feed on the skin and blubber of living southern right whales *Eubalaena australis* off Península Valdés, Argentina. The whales respond strongly to kelp gull micropredation by changing their immediate behavior during attacks and their overall surfacing pattern and body posture to minimize gull exposure. We quantified acute and chronic behavioral responses by comparing respiration rates, swim speed, and nursing behavior of southern right whale mother-calf pairs at Península Valdés with undisturbed mother-calf pairs at Head of Bight, Australia using unmanned aerial vehicle focal follows.
